# Building a Recruitment Business on the Combined Heat & Power (CHP) Market

**A full commercial breakdown of the MarketsandMarkets report "Combined Heat and Power Market — Global Forecast to 2030" and how to convert it into a specialist recruitment firm.**

---

## 1. What the Report Says (The Market Fundamentals)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market size (2025) | USD 32.02 billion |
| Market size (2030) | USD 41.48 billion |
| Growth (CAGR 2025–2030) | 5.3% |
| Net new market value created | ~USD 9.5 billion over 5 years |
| Fastest-growing region | Asia Pacific |
| Largest prime mover segment | Reciprocating engines |
| Fastest-growing prime mover | Microturbines (compact, high-efficiency, space-constrained sites) |
| Largest end-user segment | Industrial (process heat & steam demand) |

**Why the market is growing (report drivers):**
- Rising demand for **energy efficiency** and reliable on-site power generation.
- Growing load on grids from urban infrastructure, industrial processes and renewables integration — aging grids push users toward decentralised generation.
- Stringent **environmental regulations** rewarding high-efficiency generation (CHP captures waste heat, reaching 70–90% total efficiency vs ~40–50% for grid power alone).
- Rapid industrialisation and electrification in developing economies.
- Emerging tailwinds not fully priced in: **data centre power demand**, hydrogen-ready engines/turbines, biogas/renewable natural gas, and district heating expansion in Europe.

**What this means for recruitment:** a $32B market growing ~$1.9B/year cannot grow without people. Every segment in the report maps directly to a hiring demand pool — engineers to design the plants, technicians to service the engines, sales teams to sell the systems, and project/asset managers to build and run them. Energy-transition engineering talent is already one of the most acute skill shortages globally, and CHP sits in a niche most generalist recruiters don't understand — which is exactly where specialist firms earn premium fees.

## 2. Translating the Report's Segments into Recruitment Niches

The report segments the market five ways. Each segmentation is a lens for building recruitment desks:

### 2.1 By Prime Mover → Your Technical Desks
| Report segment | Recruitment niche | Typical roles |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocating engines** (largest share) | Gas engine OEMs & service networks | Field service engineers, commissioning engineers, application engineers, service sales |
| **Gas turbines** | Turbine OEMs, utilities, large industrial | Turbine controls engineers, performance engineers, outage managers, LTSA managers |
| **Steam turbines** | Heavy industry, biomass/EfW plants | Rotating equipment engineers, plant engineers |
| **Fuel cells** (high-growth, clean-tech adjacent) | Fuel cell manufacturers, hydrogen economy | Electrochemists, stack engineers, power electronics engineers, BD managers |
| **Microturbines** (fastest growth) | Distributed/modular energy vendors | Product engineers, channel sales, service techs |

### 2.2 By Capacity → Client Size & Deal Type
- **Up to 10 MW** — commercial buildings, hospitals, universities, small industrial. High unit volume → high volume of installer/service technician hiring. Contract and perm tech recruitment.
- **11–150 MW** — the industrial sweet spot: food & beverage, chemicals, pulp & paper, pharma. Plant engineers, energy managers, EPC project teams.
- **151–300 MW and above 300 MW** — utility-scale district energy and large industrial cogeneration. Fewer, bigger clients; executive search and project leadership territory (project directors, O&M directors).

### 2.3 By End User → Your Client Verticals
| End user | Who hires | Roles in demand |
|---|---|---|
| **Industrial (largest)** | Manufacturers, chemicals, refineries, food processing | Energy managers, CHP plant operators, maintenance/reliability engineers, EHS |
| **Utilities** | District energy companies, municipal utilities, IPPs | Asset managers, operations directors, grid interconnection engineers |
| **Commercial** | Hospitals, universities, data centres, hotels, real estate | Energy services sales, facilities/energy engineers, ESCO project managers |
| **Residential** | District heating operators, micro-CHP vendors | Installation engineers, heat network designers |

### 2.4 By Fuel → Future-Proofing the Business
Natural gas dominates today, but the growth stories — **biogas/biomethane, hydrogen-ready equipment, waste heat** — pull in clean-energy candidates and clients, letting you position as an "energy transition" recruiter rather than a legacy fossil recruiter. This matters for brand, candidate attraction and long-term defensibility.

### 2.5 By Region → Your Geographic Roadmap
- **Europe** (strong CHP heritage: Germany, UK, Netherlands, Scandinavia) — district heating mandates, decarbonisation policy, EU energy-efficiency directives. Mature client base, high fee tolerance. **Best launch market if you're UK/EU-based.**
- **North America** — industrial cogeneration, resilience/microgrid demand, data centre boom driving on-site generation.
- **Asia Pacific** (fastest-growing per the report) — China, India, Japan, South Korea. Enter later via RPO/partnerships or serve APAC expansion hires of Western OEMs.

## 3. Your Target Client Universe (Business Development Map)

### Tier 1 — OEMs named in/around the report (large accounts, PSL-driven)
GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Wärtsilä, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (incl. MHIET), Veolia, Caterpillar (Cat Electric Power / Solar Turbines), Cummins, INNIO Jenbacher, MAN Energy Solutions, Rolls-Royce Power Systems (MTU), Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Capstone Green Energy, 2G Energy, Yanmar, Doosan, Bosch (thermotechnology/large CHP), Viessmann, Bloom Energy and other fuel cell players.

*Reality check:* these giants have internal talent teams and preferred supplier lists. They're 12–24 month relationship plays — target them for hard-to-fill niche roles (field service, controls, hydrogen) where PSL agencies fail, and for contract/temp on outages and commissioning.

### Tier 2 — The sweet spot: mid-market (fastest wins, least competition)
- **CHP packagers/installers & ESCOs** (energy service companies selling heat-and-power-as-a-service)
- **Independent service providers** maintaining engine/turbine fleets
- **District energy developers and operators**
- **Biogas/anaerobic digestion and energy-from-waste operators**
- **EPC contractors and owner's engineers** delivering CHP/cogen projects
- **Data centre and campus energy developers** adding on-site generation

These firms have 20–500 staff, no internal recruiters, chronic engineer shortages, and will pay 20–25% fees for someone who speaks their language.

### Tier 3 — End users
Industrial manufacturers, hospitals/NHS trusts, universities, food & beverage plants hiring energy managers and plant operations staff. Lower volume per client but huge in aggregate, and often retained-search friendly for senior energy roles.

## 5. Business Model & Revenue Plan

### Recommended model: hybrid desk
1. **Contingent perm (core, months 1–12):** 20–25% of salary, engineers and sales roles for Tier 2 clients. Fast cash flow.
2. **Contract/temp (build from month 6):** field service and commissioning contractors for outages, projects and service backlogs. Requires funding for payroll (use an invoice-finance/umbrella back office initially). This becomes the stable base of the business.
3. **Retained/exec search (opportunistic):** leadership roles for ESCOs, developers and scale-ups. 30% fees, ⅓ upfront.
4. **Later (year 2+):** embedded/RPO deals with growing packagers; APAC/US market-entry hiring projects for European OEMs.

### Illustrative 3-year P&L trajectory (solo founder → small team)
| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perm placements | 15–20 | 35–45 | 60–80 |
| Avg fee | £12k | £13k | £14k |
| Contractors out (avg) | 3 | 10 | 20 |
| Revenue (NFI) | £200–290k | £600–800k | £1.1–1.6m |
| Headcount | 1–2 | 3–5 | 6–9 |

Startup costs are modest: CRM (Vincere/Bullhorn/Loxo ~£100–300/user/mo), LinkedIn Recruiter (~£8–10k/yr), job boards/CV databases, company formation, a website — realistically **£15–25k in year 1** before your own salary.

## 6. Go-to-Market Plan

### First 90 days
1. **Pick the beachhead:** gas engine/reciprocating CHP service & commissioning engineers for mid-market packagers, ESCOs and independent service firms in your home market (e.g. UK/DACH). It's the largest segment with the worst talent shortage.
2. **Build the market map:** list every CHP packager, ESCO, engine distributor, biogas operator and district energy company in the territory (trade bodies help: the ADE — Association for Decentralised Energy (UK), COGEN Europe, CHP Alliance (US), plus WPE/Energy Institute directories).
3. **Build the candidate pool first:** 300–500 mapped engineers (LinkedIn, engine-course alumni, ex-forces engineering leavers, gas network technicians). In shortage markets, *candidates are the product* — MPC (most-placeable-candidate) marketing opens client doors faster than cold BD.
4. **Content and presence:** a simple site + weekly LinkedIn posts on CHP hiring/salary data; publish a **CHP & Decentralised Energy Salary Guide** — the single best lead magnet in a niche this size.
5. **Attend the trade shows:** e.g. Data Centre World, EnerGaia/E-world, All-Energy, Hydrogen + fuel cell expos, ADE events — the industry is small and relationship-driven.

### Differentiators to hammer
- You know the difference between a Jenbacher 420 and an SGT-400 — generalists don't.
- Salary benchmarking data for CHP roles (nobody publishes this niche).
- Cross-border reach into the fastest-growing corridors (EU→US data centre power, EU→APAC OEM expansion).
- Energy-transition framing: hydrogen-ready, biogas, heat networks — attracts both candidates and clean-tech clients.

## 7. Risks & Mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| CHP is gas-linked; long-term decarbonisation pressure on fossil CHP | Anchor brand in "decentralised energy" (CHP + heat networks + biogas + hydrogen + fuel cells) so the desk migrates with the market |
| 5.3% CAGR is steady, not explosive | The recruitment opportunity is driven by *talent scarcity*, not just market growth — an aging engineer workforce retiring out is a bigger driver than the CAGR |
| Big OEM PSLs are hard to crack | Lead with mid-market; enter OEMs via contract/outage staffing and impossible-to-fill niches |
| Solo-founder cash flow gaps in contingent perm | Push for retained/exclusive terms early; add contract book by month 6–9 |
| Regional demand shifts (APAC fastest-growing) | Serve APAC indirectly first via Western OEM expansion hires; partner locally before opening offices |

## 8. KPIs to Run the Business On
- Placements/month and average fee (targets: 1–2/mo by month 6 at £12k+)
- Contractors out on billing (target: 5 by month 12)
- Client mix: no client >25% of NFI
- Candidate pool: mapped engineers in CRM (target 1,000 by month 12) and response rate to outreach (>30% in a niche this tight)
- Ratio metrics: CVs sent → interview (≥60%), interview → placement (≥1:3) — in a true specialist niche these ratios should be far better than generalist recruitment

## 9. Bottom Line

The report describes a **$32B → $41.5B market** whose growth is gated by a shrinking, aging pool of gas engine, turbine and energy systems talent. The recruitment play is:

1. **Niche down** to reciprocating-engine/packaged CHP service & projects talent (largest segment, worst shortage).
2. **Sell to the mid-market** (packagers, ESCOs, service firms, district energy developers) where there are no PSLs.
3. **Build a contract book** on outages and commissioning for recurring revenue.
4. **Brand as decentralised/clean energy** so the business rides the hydrogen, biogas and heat-network transition rather than being stranded with gas.
5. **Expand along the report's own map:** Europe first, North America (data centre power) second, APAC (fastest growth) via partnerships.

A focused solo launch can realistically reach **£200–300k NFI in year 1** and scale to a **£1m+ NFI boutique by year 3** — at typical recruitment-sector multiples, that's a genuinely valuable asset built on a market most recruiters have never heard of.
