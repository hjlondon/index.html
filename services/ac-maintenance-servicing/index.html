What's included

A service visit is a checklist, not a glance. Each unit gets:

  • Filter clean or replacement — the single biggest cause of lost cooling capacity and high running costs
  • Indoor and outdoor coil cleaning — restores heat-exchange efficiency and cuts energy use
  • Condensate pump and drain treatment — prevents the leaks that stain ceilings and close rooms

  • Electrical and controls check — terminals, contactors, sensors and error-code history
  • Written service report and compliance certificate — emailed after every visit, ready for auditors, insurers and landlords

Frequently asked questions

How often does commercial air conditioning legally need servicing?

The F-Gas Regulation sets minimum leak-check frequencies based on refrigerant charge: annually from 5 tonnes CO2-equivalent, every six months from 50 tonnes, and quarterly from 500 tonnes. Beyond the legal minimum, manufacturers require regular servicing to keep warranties valid — for most commercial systems that means at least two visits a year.

What does F-Gas compliance actually require from me?

If your system contains 5 tonnes CO2-equivalent of refrigerant or more, you must have it leak-checked at the prescribed frequency by certified engineers and keep records of checks, repairs and refrigerant added or recovered. We carry out the checks, maintain the register and hand you the evidence.

What happens if you find a fault during a service?

You get a written report with a fixed-price repair quote — we don't fix and invoice without asking. Minor items like a blocked drain or a loose terminal are usually sorted on the spot within the visit.

Do you take over systems you didn't install?

Yes. Most of our maintenance portfolio was installed by someone else. The first visit includes a condition survey of each unit so you know exactly what you've inherited before you commit to a contract.

Can you cover multiple sites under one contract?

Yes — multi-site portfolios across South East London and Kent are core work for us. One contract, one renewal date, per-site reporting, and the same engineers attending each site so they know the plant.
