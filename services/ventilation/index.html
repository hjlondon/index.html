Frequently asked questions

What ventilation do Building Regulations require for commercial premises?

Part F of the Building Regulations sets minimum fresh-air rates — broadly 10 litres per second per person for offices, with higher and use-specific rates for kitchens, sanitary accommodation and clinical rooms. We calculate the requirement for your occupancy and design to it, and the commissioning figures prove it's met.

Does a commercial kitchen legally need extract ventilation?

Yes — a kitchen producing hot food needs mechanical extract through a canopy sized to the cooking equipment beneath it, with adequate make-up air. Insurers additionally expect grease filtration and regular duct cleaning records under TR19 guidance; we design and install to support both.

How often should ventilation ductwork be cleaned?

Kitchen extract ductwork should be cleaned to TR19 intervals based on cooking hours — heavily used kitchens as often as every three months, light use annually. General office ductwork needs inspection roughly every couple of years. Dirty extract duct is a documented fire risk and a common insurance exclusion.

Can ventilation run alongside air conditioning?

It should — air conditioning recirculates and ventilation replaces. A heat-recovery unit brings in fresh air while reclaiming most of the energy from the outgoing air, so the two systems work together instead of fighting each other. We design and install both, which keeps the controls coherent.
